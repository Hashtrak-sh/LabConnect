# Search

**Status:** Approved at gate G2 (2026-11-25).

## 1. Index design (OpenSearch)
Index `lab_scope_v{n}` (alias `lab_scope`). **One document per active scope line** at a listed site, denormalised:
```
scope_line_id, lab_site_id, lab_org_id, site_name, site_code, city, state_code, pincode,
location (geo_point), listing_status, is_bookable, lab_category, accreditation_status, accreditation_valid_to,
discipline{code,name}, group{…}, product{…,aliases}, parameter{…,aliases}, method{code,name,standard_code},
range_text, site_type, price_paise (nullable), tat_days, temporarily_unavailable,
rating_avg, rating_count, responsiveness_score, next_slot_at, searchable_attributes{...}
```
- Analysers: standard + lowercase + ASCII folding, an edge n-gram sub-field for typeahead, a **synonym graph** filter fed from `search_synonym` (e.g. `TS ⇒ tensile strength`, `IS 1786 ⇒ TMT bars`), and fuzziness `AUTO` on the main query.
- Queries use `collapse` on `lab_site_id`, with `inner_hits` returning the matched scope lines (the "why this lab matched" view, FR-DIS-05), a `geo_distance` filter, and a sort by distance or relevance.
- Only public reference data is indexed (TENANT_ISOLATION §4).

## 2. Sync
- Event-driven: `masterdata.published`, `pricelist.published`, `lab_site.bookability_changed`, `slot.capacity_changed` (debounced), `rating.updated` → `lc-search-sync` → upsert or delete by `scope_line_id` / `lab_site_id`.
- Nightly full rebuild into a new index version, then the alias is swapped (backstop and reindex path).
- Freshness target: < 60 s from publication to search (AC-MDM-03, AC-DIS-05).

## 3. Fallback
`SearchService` has a Postgres implementation (`pg_trgm` + PostGIS) for local development, tests and degraded mode. Search is behind an interface, so OpenSearch adoption can be revisited (ADR-007) if Phase 3 benchmarks show Postgres meets NFR-03 at year-1 scale.

### Matching and scoring (as built, OQ-246)
The rules are the same in the three backends and are stated once in `packages/core/src/search/word-match.ts`; the in-memory index uses that file as it is, `PostgresSearchIndex` repeats it in SQL (`matching-sql.ts`) and OpenSearch approximates it with weighted clauses (`opensearch-dsl.ts`).

- **What a word is.** The text is lower-cased and cut at every separator: white space, ASCII punctuation and symbols (so brackets, hyphens, slashes, full stops, underscores, `%`, `*` and `\` all separate words), the Latin-1 punctuation and symbols, the general punctuation block (U+2000 to U+206F), CJK punctuation (U+3000 to U+303F) and the Devanagari danda. Letters, digits and combining marks of every script stay inside a word. Examples: "pH value" is `ph`, `value`; "Tensile strength (UTS)" is `tensile`, `strength`, `uts`; "UTS/YS ratio" is `uts`, `ys`, `ratio`; "Total hardness (as CaCO3)" is `total`, `hardness`, `as`, `caco3`; "E. coli" is `e`, `coli`; "IS 3025 (Part 11)" is `is`, `3025`, `part`, `11`. The query is cut the same way. A query that has no word (`(((`, `%`, `.*`) matches nothing, and no character of it is ever a pattern: the Postgres code compares words with `=` and `position()`, never with `LIKE` or a regular expression built from the query.
- **How a query word matches a text.** A word of one to three characters matches only a whole word: `pH` finds the word "pH" and not "Phosphorus", `LED` finds "LED lamps" and not "filled", `TS` finds a line only through its synonym expansion (AC-DIS-02) or a word `TS`. A word of four characters or more matches a whole word or the beginning of a word: `tens` finds "tensile", `chlor` finds "chloride", `sulph` finds "sulphate". Never the middle or the end: `loride`, `ensile` and `mad` (in "Narmada") find nothing. A word of four characters or more is also forgiven a typing mistake (same first letter, one edit, two from eight letters; Postgres: `word_similarity` of 0.6 and a length within two): `strenght` finds "strength", `tensil strenght` finds the tensile strength lines.
- **Several words.** Before, the whole text was one phrase that had to occur as a substring (plus one trigram comparison of the whole phrase). Now a line matches when **every** word of the query matches it, in any order and in any of its texts, so "strength" alone on a compressive strength line does not make a site appear as if it offered "tensile strength". A synonym expansion replaces the words it stands for (`TS` becomes the alternative term "tensile strength"), and the best alternative counts. Words such as "of" or "the" are not ignored: "tensile strength of steel" needs a line with the word "of" (OQ-255).
- **Grade of a word on a line**, best first: 5 a whole word of the parameter's name; 4 the beginning of a word of the parameter's name; 3 a whole word or the beginning of a word of the product's name; 2 the same in any other text (parameter and product aliases, group, discipline, method name, standard code, method text); 1 a typing mistake against any of them; 0 no match. Tier 3 of the original rule (product, group, discipline, method, alias) is split in two so that "cement" lists a test of a cement product before "Slump", whose product only has the alias "cement concrete".
- **Score of a line**, 0 to 1: the best alternative of `0` if any word has grade 0; below 0.25 if any word has grade 1 (so a typing mistake is always below any real match); otherwise `0.4 + 0.6 * (0.7 * (lowest - 2) / 3 + 0.3 * (average - 2) / 3)` over the grades of its words. One word: 1.0 name word, 0.8 name prefix, 0.6 product name, 0.4 other text. More matching words in the name raise the average.
- **Site name and code** follow the same rules (grades 5, 4 and 1 only): `nar` does not find "Narmada", `narm` does, `mad` does not. A site found by name only has no matched line.
- **Order.** The matched lines of a site are listed by score, best first (ties by line id). The site's relevance is the best of its line scores and its name score. With the relevance sort, sites are ordered by it, then by id. With the distance, price and turnaround sorts nothing changes except the tie-break: sites with the same value come best match first, then by id (a cursor carries the score for it; on OpenSearch the sort has `_score` after the key, which scores the top line of the site for that sort, so there it is approximate).
- **Postgres cost.** The text is matched against the visible lines of all sites, as before (there is no text index: the trigram index idea of section 3 was never built, and the visible-lines query reads every public line). The words of each distinct text (a few hundred for thousands of lines) are cut once, a site's name is scored once per distinct text and the words a typing mistake could mean are looked up once over the distinct words of the data, so a line costs a few `position()` calls. The query reads its own `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE)` as a sequential read of the visible lines (the joins of `visible-lines.ts`, which dominate) followed by a scan of the scored lines: no index serves the text, before or after this change. See OQ-254 for what grows with the data.
- **OpenSearch.** Per word, a `dis_max` of constant-score clauses (whole word in the name 5, prefix 4, product 3, other 2, `fuzziness: AUTO:4,8` with `prefix_length: 1` 1); a term is a `bool` with one `must` per word; the terms are alternatives. The standard tokenizer cuts a few things differently from the rule above (it keeps `2.5` and `a_b` as one word and ASCII-folds accents, and the prefix clause does not fold the query), so results can differ on such text (OQ-256). Not verified against a cluster.

## 4. As built (P3-10)
Code: `packages/core/src/search/` (framework-free), `apps/worker/src/modules/search/` (jobs), `apps/api/src/modules/search/` (providers and the response mapper). `searchLabs` is served by `SearchController` (I1-CUS-01): the query string is parsed in `search.dto.ts` (unknown or repeated parameters are 422, so `sort` stays hidden, OQ-151), a pincode is resolved by the `PincodeLocator` port, whose only implementation knows no pincode yet (every pincode is 422 "not known", OQ-153), and the result is mapped by `toLabSearchResultList`. `suggestSearch` is I1-CUS-08.

- **Port.** `SearchIndex` has `ensureIndex`, `search`, `upsertDocuments`, `deleteDocuments`, `replaceSites` (makes the index hold exactly the given documents of a site; a site with none is removed), `listSiteIds` and `isHealthy`. Adapters: `PostgresSearchIndex` (reads the tables, the fallback and the default when `OPENSEARCH_URL` is unset), `OpenSearchSearchIndex`, and `InMemorySearchIndex` (the fake, and the executable statement of the query semantics). One conformance suite (`test/db/search-conformance.ts`) runs against Postgres and the in-memory index with every `pnpm test:db`, and against a real OpenSearch with `pnpm --filter @labconnect/core test:opensearch`.
- **Query.** `normaliseSearchQuery` validates like the contract (422 `VALIDATION_FAILED`) into a `SearchQuery`: text, `lat`/`lng`, `radiusKm` (5, 10, 25, 50, 100), `discipline`, `parameter`, `method`, `tatMax`, `priceMax` (paise, bigint), `category`, `sort` (`relevance`, `distance`, `price`, `tat`: OQ-151), `limit`, `cursor`, and the instant `asOf` from the injected clock. Filters combine with AND. The text and its synonym expansions (`expandTerms`, from `search_synonym`) are the `terms` every backend searches. A line matches by its parameter, product, group, discipline, method and standard text and aliases, by whole words and the beginning of words, every word of the query having to match (section 3, "Matching and scoring"); a site matches by its name or code. A site found by name only has no matched line (the contract).
- **Document.** One per public scope line (`LabSearchDocument`): site (code, name, city, state, pincode, approved pin only, listing status, rating aggregate), accreditation (certificate, body, standard, validity, verification URL template), the scope line (discipline, group, product, parameter, method with standard code, range text, site type, temporarily unavailable), `isBookable`, and `pricePaise`, `tatDays`, `nextSlotAt` only for a bookable site. Never: tenant ids, contact details, bookability reasons, capacity or slot counts, responsiveness (OQ-150). `deny-list.ts` mirrors the contract's deny-list and tests hold the document, the index mapping and the response to it.
- **Which lines are public** (`visible-lines.ts`, the single definition used by the projector and by Postgres search; rules and reasons in OQ-155): site `LISTED` or `BOOKABLE` and `ACTIVE`; organisation `ACTIVE`; line `ACTIVE` and in its dates; accreditation `VALID` and in date at `asOf` (BR-002), category `TESTING` or `CALIBRATION` (BR-005). Price, turnaround and slot only when bookable at `asOf` (BR-001). Distance only from an approved pin (BR-004). The accreditation dates are also checked at query time by every adapter, so an expired certificate drops out at once without waiting for the nightly job.
- **Database context.** Public search and the projector run in `withPublicReadContext`: a read-only, repeatable-read transaction with all four RLS settings empty, so only public reference data is visible and nothing can be written (OQ-154). It is not the system context. `search` reads other modules' tables in one file (OQ-152).
- **Result.** `SiteSearchResult` has exactly what `LabSearchResult` of the contract needs. `priceFrom` and `minTatDays` are taken over the matched lines, or over the whole price list when none matched. `apps/api/.../search-response.ts` maps it to `LabSearchResultList`; `test/search-contract.spec.ts` validates the mapped response with the contract validator.
- **Fallback.** `SearchService` answers from OpenSearch when `OPENSEARCH_URL` is set and the circuit is closed, and from Postgres otherwise or on an error or a timeout (default 1.5 s) of the same request. Two failures in a row open the circuit for 30 s; then one request tests OpenSearch again. Cursors name the backend that issued them (`pg1.`, `os1.`): a cursor is never used on the other backend, and one that cannot be honoured is a 422 "start again". Nothing logs the query text or a cursor (OQ-40: the infrastructure must also keep `lat` and `lng` out of access logs).
- **OpenSearch.** Index `lab_scope_v1` behind the alias `lab_scope` (`OPENSEARCH_INDEX_ALIAS`, `OPENSEARCH_INDEX_VERSION`), `dynamic: strict`, `geo_point`, keyword filters, `lc_text` (lower-case, ASCII folding) and an edge n-gram sub-field. Queries collapse on `lab_site_id` with inner hits for the matched lines, the cheapest and fastest line and one line per accreditation. Documents carry the projection time as an external version, so a late older projection never overwrites a newer one. Differences from the sections above: OQ-157. Auth: `none` or `basic`; AWS SigV4 is refused (OQ-156).
- **Sync.** `lc-search-sync` carries `masterdata.published`, `pricelist.published`, `lab_site.bookability_changed`, `slot.capacity_changed`, `rating.updated`, `accreditation.changed` and `lab.became_bookable` (`platform/routes.ts`). `handleSearchSyncEvent` reads only site ids from the event, re-projects those sites from the database and calls `replaceSites`: idempotent and order-insensitive. See OQ-158 for what emitters must send. `reindexAll` (batched, resumable by cursor) projects every visible site, then removes indexed sites that are no longer visible; the worker runs it at start (`SEARCH_REINDEX_ON_START=true`) and on an interval (`SEARCH_REINDEX_INTERVAL_MS`). The worker starts the search jobs only with `WORKER_DATABASE_URL` and `OPENSEARCH_URL`; the consumer also needs `QUEUE_URL_LC_SEARCH_SYNC`.
- **Not built.** Typeahead (`suggestSearch`), a pincode dataset for `PincodeLocator` (OQ-153), the nightly rebuild into a new index version with an alias swap (OQ-157), SigV4 (OQ-156), a Redis rate limit (OQ-88).
