# UX principles

1. **One clear next action.** Every booking screen shows its state, who it's waiting on, and the single primary action for this user ("Pay ₹3,540 by 4:30 PM IST").
2. **Task-oriented dashboards.** Dashboards open on "Needs your action" queues, not on charts. Counts link to filtered lists.
3. **Show the why.** Search results show which scope lines matched. Rejections show the reason. Non-bookable labs show why ("Listed – not yet on LabConnect", "Calibration booking coming soon").
4. **Minimal cognitive load.** Progressive disclosure in the TRF. Smart defaults (customer site, package count from quantities). Never more than one decision per step.
5. **Trust signals everywhere.** Accreditation status with an as-of date, certificate number, NABL verification link, approval trail ("Received by Sanjay (Maker) · approved by Dr. Mehta (Checker)").
6. **Errors that help.** Say what happened, why, and what to do. Never just "Error 422". Map API error codes to messages.
7. **Time is explicit.** Deadlines show both the absolute time (IST) and the relative time ("in 3 h"). SLA timers are visible to the party who must act.
8. **Separation of duties is visible.** The UI explains why a Checker can't approve their own record, instead of silently hiding the button.
9. **Counter-first design for labs.** Big touch targets, the camera scanner as the primary action, an offline indicator, and quick photo capture.
10. **Accessible by default.** Colour plus icon plus text for status, focus states, sufficient contrast, form labels.
11. **Respect privacy.** Mask phone numbers, never show other tenants' data, and show the verification page's limited data clearly.
